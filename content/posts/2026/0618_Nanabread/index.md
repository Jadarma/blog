Quick and easy banana bread recipe you can mix in a single bowl and cook in an instant pot.

<!--more-->

# Straight To The Point

**🍌 Wet Mix**
- Ripe bananas _(x4-6)_
- Eggs _(x2)_
- Olive Oil _(120ml)_
- Milk _(60ml)_

**🧂 Powders**
- Ground Cinnamon _(10g / 2 tsp)_
- Baking Powder _(10g / 2 tsp)_
- Baking Soda _(10g / 2 tsp)_
- Flour _(240g)_
- Salt _(a pinch)_

**🍫 Fillers (Suggestive)**
- Oats _(50g)_
- Vanilla sugar / extract.
- Honey
- Seeds, nuts, chocolate, etc.

# The Image Guide

## Mise en place

This is the list of ingredients
_(keen observers will note I didn't end up using the butter)_.
Select your ripest bananas, mine were stored in the fridge so they browned less.

{{< figure src="nanabread0.webp" alt="Prepared ingredients." >}}

Start by grabbing a mixing bowl and adding the peeled bananas to it.
Use a masher to break them apart in a paste;
if they are really ripe it's easy to do this with a fork also.

Add all the other wet ingredients first: the olive oil, milk, and eggs.
Stir until thoroughly combined.

{{< figure src="nanabread1.webp" alt="Wet ingredients added together to a bowl." >}}

Then add the powdered ingredients: baking powder, baking soda, ground cinnamon, and a pinch of salt.
Here I also added a small pack of vanilla sugar, but if you have extract, use that.

{{< figure src="nanabread2.webp" alt="Powders and spices added to the mixture." >}}

Then add the flower and oats on top and mix again.

{{< figure src="nanabread3.webp" alt="Flower and oats added." >}}

Here is how the base mix should look like so far.

{{< figure src="nanabread4.webp" alt="Reference base mixture consistency." >}}

From here, you can add whatever fillings you desire.
I used diced dark chocholate and some pumpkin seeds.
To offset the bitterness from the chockolate, I also added a few squeezes of honey.

Other good choices include walnuts and peanut butter.

{{< figure src="nanabread5.webp" alt="Adding the custom fillings." >}}

With the mixture ready, it is time to transfer to the pot.
Oil up the pot's walls to prevent sticking, either with more olive oil or non-stick spray.
To be extra cautious, I also cut a circle of parchment paper to place at the bottom.

Transfer the mixture over it, distribute it evenly, and sprinkle some more oats and stuff on top for decoration.

{{< figure src="nanabread6.webp" alt="Final look before transferring to the pot." >}}

Cooking instructions depend on what model you have, in my case, it was a pressure cooker that had a _Dessert_ mode, so
I used that at low pressure for about 40 minutes.
This can be done in a normal rice cooker too, or an oven.

Check in on it after 30-35 minutes, or when the scent of cinnamon becomes tempting, and do a toothpick test: stab it
and if the pick comes out clean, you should be close to finished.

Here is how mine turned out.
Leave to rest and cool for 15-20 minutes.

{{< figure src="nanabread7.webp" alt="View from the pot during the toothpick test." >}}

To get it out, I used a couple of plates that fit in nicely inside the pot, turned the pot upside down, lift gently,
then using the second plate turn it upright again.

{{< figure src="nanabread8.webp" alt="Ready to serve banana bread." >}}

The result is a very soft, flavorful but not overly sweet banana bread.
Works great with morning coffee.

Enjoy! 🍞
